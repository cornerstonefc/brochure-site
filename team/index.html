About Dr. Heather Hallam-Helbich

Dr. Hallam-Helbich considers it an absolute privilege and joy to work with clients ages 3 to 25 to support them and their parents collaboratively. She brings more than 30 years of experience working with people from a range of cultural and socioeconomic backgrounds. In her work, she incorporates therapeutic approaches, play therapy techniques, and hands-on activities to support cognitive and behavioural change, renewed confidence, and growth.

Dr. Hallam-Helbich completes thorough assessments to identify clients’ cognitive, academic, socio-emotional, and behavioural needs. She helps clarify diagnoses and provides appropriate referrals so clients and parents can advocate for themselves or their children. She helps individuals obtain educational plans that support them in reaching their potential and provides parents with behaviour management strategies and support for addressing difficult issues at home.

About Dr. Amy Tran

Dr. Amy Tran strongly believes that every person’s story deserves careful attention. How we understand a concern can shape how we respond to it. She takes time to understand each person’s unique experiences, strengths, relationships, and developmental needs to create a safe space where youth and families feel heard, understood, and supported.

Dr. Tran completed her Ph.D. in child and adolescent clinical psychology. Her specific training provides a strong foundation in child and adolescent development and in understanding young people’s challenges within the context of their stage of life, family, and school environment. She provides therapy and assessment services to clients with a warm, thoughtful, and collaborative approach.

Dr. Tran’s previous clinical experiences include the Regional Children’s Centre, The Summit Centre for Preschool Children with Autism, the Toronto Catholic District School Board, Grand River Hospital, and Youthdale Treatment Centres. In therapy, she employs evidence-based strategies to help clients navigate difficult emotions, strengthen relationships, and build confidence in themselves. Her work also incorporates nervous system regulation strategies to support the mind-body connection and help people feel calmer and more in control. She helps families make sense of assessment findings and understand their next steps, including how recommendations can inform educators and support teams.

Dr. Tran is also the author of This Book Is a Safe Space. She has served on advisory boards for mental health apps, appeared as a guest on wellness podcasts, provided workshops to various organizations, and published work in peer-reviewed research journals, magazines, and news publications. Across her clinical work and writing, she is committed to helping young people and their families feel more understood and confident about the path forward.

About Elizabeth Allevato

Elizabeth has always known that she wanted to work in a helping profession. Kindness, compassion, and collaboration are at the core of her work. She believes we all benefit when we support one another in becoming the best versions of ourselves, and she strives to create a therapeutic environment where clients feel understood, respected, and empowered.

With more than 15 years of experience in both the public and private sectors, Elizabeth has developed a strong background in assessment and intervention. Much of her training has focused on Intellectual Developmental Disorder, Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder, Specific Learning Disorders, and anxiety disorders. More recently, she has been expanding her knowledge of mood and personality disorders as they relate to assessment and therapeutic support. Although assessments are the primary focus of her practice, she also provides therapy to a smaller caseload of children, adolescents, and adults.

Elizabeth’s approach is collaborative, flexible, and grounded in evidence-based practices. She wants clients to feel heard and validated while gently exploring patterns of thinking or behaviour that may be getting in the way of their goals. She believes therapy works best when it meets people where they are, so she adapts sessions to each person’s strengths, needs, and circumstances. With children, therapy often focuses on practical skills for emotional regulation, cognitive flexibility, attention, and impulse control. With adolescents and adults, sessions are more collaborative, with clients actively helping to set the agenda and develop their goals.

About Kimberly Court

Kim believes that when life feels overwhelming, heartbreaking, or challenging, people possess inner resources that can help them move forward. She also recognizes that doing this work alone is difficult and that being vulnerable can feel scary. Having someone to navigate these seasons with can make a profound difference.

Kim recognizes that clients are the experts in their own lives. She works collaboratively with them to identify personal goals, reconnect with their innate strengths, and build the skills they need to move forward. Her hope is that clients leave their sessions feeling more confident, emotionally healthy, and empowered.

Kim tailors her approach to each individual or couple. She draws on a range of evidence-based approaches, including grief and loss therapy, strengths-based and narrative approaches, cognitive and dialectical behavioural therapies, solution-focused therapy, Gottman methods, and Collaborative Problem Solving. She is also a trained facilitator with Circle of Security International and provides reflective, attachment-based parenting support.

Above all, Kim wants clients to feel safe, heard, and validated. She offers an empathetic, compassionate, and nonjudgmental space where they can share their stories and begin their healing journey.

About Levi Rogers

Levi is a Registered Social Worker with experience supporting youth, adults, and families in various clinical settings since 2015. He began practicing as an individual and family therapist in 2020. He has worked at local mental health agencies which helped him to establish a strong clinical foundation and knowledge of supports available in the community to youth and their families. He graduated with a Master of Social Work degree from the University of Windsor and has an Ontario College Advanced Diploma in Child and Youth Care (CYC/CYW) and Developmental Services Worker (DSW).

Accredited as a Triple-P (Positive Parenting Program) facilitator, Levi has experience supporting parents and families with treating behavioral and emotional challenges experienced by children and youth.

Levi is trained in several therapeutic treatment modalities. He takes a collaborative, trauma-informed and anti-oppressive approach to supporting clients in meeting their personal goals. Levi is a compassionate, engaging, and skilled clinician who prioritizes empathy and respect for his clients. He recognizes that attending therapy may not come easy to each person, and therefore is committed to building a comfortable and safe environment where clients feel seen, heard, and valued.

Levi teaches part-time in St. Clair College’s Developmental Services Worker program, which trains professionals to support people with intellectual and developmental disabilities throughout their lives. Graduates work as support workers, advocates, or educational assistants. He enjoys teaching developmental psychology, positive behavioural supports, interpersonal counselling, dual diagnosis, and mental health intervention.

About Taryn Duffy

Taryn believes connection between client and therapist is the backbone of therapy. She builds strong rapport with her clients by offering a comforting, kind and grounded space, where clients can feel safe to unpack some of life's heavier moments. Her goal is for each person to feel heard, understood, and supported.

Taryn works with youth, teens, adults, and seniors through a range of life experiences, including anxiety, trauma, grief, emotional regulation challenges, and periods of personal growth and self-discovery. She also has experience supporting neurodivergent clients.

She uses a multi-modal approach, pulling from bottom-up approaches like somatic-based, IFS, and EMDR therapy, as well as top-down approaches, such as DBT and CBT and narrative therapy.

Whether someone feels stuck, isolated, or overwhelmed, or is ready to make a change, Taryn works alongside them to explore their strengths and move toward their goals.
